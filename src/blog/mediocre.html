Be Mediocre

The beauty of hand built.

We are always told that you learn more from mistakes than from success. What does this mean? and how does it relate to how we use AI? I believe that AI has the (addictive) ability to serve you "exactly" what you are looking for in a short time span without much cognition debt from the user. This is reducing the number of mistakes the human population will make (note: human population making mistakes, AI can still make mistakes). Definitely a good thing for high risk situations but for our continued learning this is a potential double edged sword.

All we are is experience. Every time a brain has an experience, a number of neurons (weights) are updated to reinforce this experience, this is learning. These weights produce paths through the brain, some paths have a high weight and are strong, others are much weaker (low weight). These low weight paths, despite being unobvious and inefficient, will still contain (potentially) interesting learnings. It has been shown that this "long tail" of paths (the lower weight ones), are better explored by "more creative" humans. Creative humans are able to link areas of their internal embedding space in non-obvious ways and this is what creativity could be. I believe that the act of making mistakes is a proxy for suboptimal path search.

Mistakes tap straight into our own unique semanitc memory structure (embedding space). In other words, mistakes help us to directly relate how two or more ideas relate to each other in the brain. They act as “signposts” or “stepping stones”, to patch unknown connections in the brain between ideas. This is a key part of learning and something that is completely unique to every person. The way ideas are embedded in my brain is completely different to yours. If my embedding space was removed from my brain and put into yours I bet you would be completely garbled until you learn my (strange) embedding space.

AI (Large Language Models - LLMs) learns in a somewhat similar way. In pre training we teach the AI about the world. We do this by giving the AI large amounts of text from the entire internet and asking it the next word (token). We then score the model based on how good it is and update the good paths to be stronger and bad paths to be weaker using a process called stochastic gradient descent (SGD) (other optimisers are available). SGD is basically a noisy method of optimising the model. This noise could be thought of as exploration of (potentially) suboptimal paths, to try and find links that are not obvious but crucial. After the model has sufficiently absorbed the internet and optimised its score, we then fine-tune the model to produce "human aligned" responses. This is called SFT (Supervised fine-tuning). Specifically we use Reinforcement Learning on Human Feedback (RLHF). RLHF has increasingly become more complicated as it is now used to make sure code that is written has few errors and reduce the number of hallucinations. Other post training methods are also available (nowadays its called RLVR - Reinforcement Learning on Verifiable Rewards, but its all very similar just with different reward signals).

Next token prediction is a probability exercise where the AI outputs the most probable next token (path) based on a world view. We lose many of the learnings embedded within the suboptimal paths of a model when only looking at the mean (or most likely) token. At inference time we struggle to see the learnings from the suboptimal paths. Once the model is in its most optimal (lowest loss, highest score) state then pursuading it to give improbable (but still logical) answers is a difficult task. In other words, asking it to service the "long tail" and be creative (or unique) while still having a good response is a very challenging, and could result in an increased number of hallucinations as we explore the fringes of the model.

Sometimes a suboptimal path may be nonsense. However, it is possible that these paths can contain niche knowledge about topics rarely discussed on the internet. This is why we are all recommended to start prompts with "act as a proffessional [insert field name here]". By conditioning the model to generate like a proffessional or expert in a particular field we are trying to service the suboptimal paths, which are only suboptimal (in this case) becuase of a lack of training data on the field, as opposed to being wrong.

One of the more interesting prompt engineering comeuppances is the inclusion of "Do not make mistakes". This has always interested me. By asking it to not make mistakes are we now servicing more robust, stronger paths through the model and getting less interesting and novel answers? I would advocate to ask your AI to make mistakes if the aim is to get responses in the long tail, but this just seems flawed.

Instead, I want to argue that we as humans cannot rely on any AI system to operate in the "long tail". We should therefore take a leaf out of our creative friends' book and try and think outside the box in ways that AI could never. The main way to do this is to make mistakes (in environments where it wont have serious consequences). Mistakes will help the brain to link ideas previously unlinked, and these new links might be very valuable and crucially unique.

With AI, every agent you spin up is identical, trained on the same data, in the same way and has similar semantic relations. Any stochasticity we experience in LLMs comes from batch processing and our own uniqueness in prompting. A danger arises when we continue to outsource our cognition to a tetrapoloy of tech companies. We are losing our individuality. If 4 different AI's run the cognition of the human race then we might be at risk of massive cognition modal collapse.

There is something truly terrifying about becoming the most likely response. I do not want to live in a world where everything feels AI made. I want the hand built feel. What a shame it will be if the future is a world where humans delegate the ability (or vulnerability) to make mistakes to AI.

One of my (least) favourite concepts is the enshitification of colour. Enshitification of colour is the idea that 40-50 years ago the world was full of a diverse range of colour. In the modern era we all want sleek "modern" designs which just so happen to be colourless and as a result we are progressively living in a more monochromatic world. I fear the same is coming true with AI. The enshitification of cognition. Where we as a population will start to develop a mode collapse as we bias our opinions and thoughts with constant use of AI to do simple tasks like summarisation (do not underestimate the bias that can be brought in by even a task as simple as summarisation).

So I leave you this this thought. Be mediocre. Learn skills by making mistakes and not being perfect. This is how we as humans can co-exist with vastly intelligent beings and crucially avoid falling into the era of modal collapse.
